Small Claims Copilot

A free tool that turns a California small claims dispute's scattered documents into a hearing-ready evidence packet that keeps what you claim separate from what your documents prove.

The unique kernel

Separating claims from proof.

Every statement is marked backed by a document (excerpt one click away) or your word only. When sources disagree, the conflict is flagged, not smoothed over. This is the prep work the lawyer never did.

The core loop

What "working" looks like

Open the app, describe the fictional contractor dispute, drop in 3–4 synthetic documents, click once. The packet looks like this:

✓ backed by
Contractor agreed to finish the deck by March 15.

contract.txt: "Work to be completed no later than March 15, 2026."

⚠ your word only
Contractor said on the phone he'd fix the railing for free.

No document supports this yet. Follow-up: do you have a text or email that mentions it?

✕ conflict
Total price: contract says $2,400, invoice says $2,800.

contract.txt: "Total price: $2,400" · invoice.txt: "Amount due: $2,800". Both shown; neither silently chosen.

The conflict flag is the wow moment. Example content is illustrative; the real demo uses synthetic documents written during the build.

Follow-up questions

Each question says why it matters for organizing the case (e.g. "the amount you claim depends on which figure is right"), never a prediction. Where California procedure is relevant, it links to a hand-picked official California Courts page with a last-checked date. The AI never writes legal citations.

Who it's for

A tech-savvy California resident with a small claims dispute, like a homeowner owed money by a contractor, whose evidence is scattered across email and their phone. Today they organize by hand, pay someone who may skip the prework, or give up.

Feel

Calm, trustworthy, organized: the opposite of court-paperwork overwhelm. Clearly an organizer, not a lawyer, with a visible not-legal-advice notice.

Why this matters

"I've had to go to small claims court twice and it sucked, was super hard, and I ended giving up out of frustration."

Gave up at serving the other party once; another time a hired lawyer "did zero prework" before contesting a red-light camera citation, and the fine went up.

The boundary

  • One fictional contractor payment dispute, synthetic documents, one-click reset to it
  • Dispute description + a few structured answers
  • Add/remove a handful of text documents (PDF if cheap)
  • AI extracts facts with excerpts; app code validates, sorts the timeline, detects date/amount conflicts
  • Packet: summary, timeline, evidence list, backed vs. your-word-only, conflicts, follow-ups; editable and printable
  • Clear-case button, not-legal-advice notice, documents treated as untrusted data
  • Serving the other party: a service plan/checklist linked to official sources (your own sticking point)
  • Other low-stakes California disputes, e.g. traffic citation contests
  • More automation of the process overall
  • Filing, arranging service, contacting the other party: real-world legal actions, far beyond 2–4 hours, risky to automate
  • Legal advice, outcome predictions, deciding who's right: it's an organizer; pretending otherwise could hurt users
  • AI-generated legal citations: unverifiable and easy to hallucinate
  • Accounts, database, deployment: not needed to prove the kernel
